# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- Timing is a real angle: SoDEX mainnet and the Builder Race are new, so a front end built early could capture early users before the field fills.
- The safety checks (book depth, slippage, size, concentration) are a coherent wedge, and the no-profit-promise stance is honest and defensible.
- Revenue is aligned with usage: a builder fee per swap, free to the user, with one approval.
- A narrow starting market exists in principle: users of one venue, SoDEX/ValueChain.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage.** The Monopoly lens looks for roughly ten times better on something that matters, a network effect, scale or a brand. Trade checks are incremental and a copycat can ship them in weeks. Nothing here gets harder to copy with time.
2. **The platform owns the customer.** SoDEX's own site and other front ends (sodexextra.xyz bots) sell to the same buyer. SoDEX could add safety checks, change builder-fee terms or restrict builders. The whole market is borrowed from one venue.
3. **No distribution.** No marketing budget, a solo builder, and no stated way the first 100 users arrive or what each costs. The Builder Race ranking is the only channel named, and it is a contest, not a customer source.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret: what do you know about SoDEX traders that others do not?
- How many active spot traders does SoDEX have today? (Open question: no number given.)
- How will the first 100 users arrive, and at what cost each?
- What stops a copycat, or SoDEX itself, in month six?
- Is there a data or network effect, such as shared trade-check history, that improves with users?
- What happens to revenue if the builder program changes or ends?
- Has a real mainnet trade, WalletConnect relay and Gemini call worked end to end? (Brief says not yet.)
- What is expected monthly swap volume, and is 3 bp enough at that volume? (Open question: no figure given.)
